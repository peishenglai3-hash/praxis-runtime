## What this document is

One row per breakpoint — 67 rows, `BP-001` … `BP-066` plus `Breakpoint P35-01` — each carrying a status drawn from the owner's brief §10 set and from nothing else: `CLOSED`, `ACTIVE`, `EVIDENCE-REQUIRED`, `DEFERRED`, `WONTFIX`. Every basis cell cites a file and a line. Where the evidence is a Chinese phrase in the record, the phrase is quoted verbatim.

It is a reading of the record, not a decision about the code. It closes nothing by itself.

**Rule 1 — the history is preserved.** No entry was renumbered, reordered, merged or deleted, and `docs/断点记录.md` was not modified to write this document. `BP-001` … `BP-063` keep their numbers. `Breakpoint P35-01` keeps its heading exactly as written, including the fact that it does not match the `## BP-0NN` pattern (`:747`); it is carried here at its position in the file, between BP-035 and BP-036.

**Rule 2 — statuses are mapped from the entry, and never upgraded.** A completed fix or correction that the record does not later contradict is `CLOSED`. An entry that says it is not fixed is not `CLOSED`, whatever the surrounding tree says. Where an entry's closure is recorded in a later entry, that later entry is cited in the basis cell. Where a later entry contradicts an earlier closure claim, the earlier claim is not honoured: BP-049 is the case (`HANDOFF.md:85` records it as closed by the recovery; `:1464` corrects that).

**Rule 3 — a missing marker is not a closure.** Fifteen entries carry no status marker at all. Each is resolved by naming the line that settles it, or is `EVIDENCE-REQUIRED`. Five of the fifteen are §9 records that happen to carry no marker — BP-020, BP-025, BP-027, BP-032, BP-035 — and are `CLOSED` as records. Of the remaining ten, eight are settled by a cited entry or by their own recorded disposition (BP-001, BP-016, BP-017, BP-024, BP-036, BP-043, BP-048, BP-062), one is `EVIDENCE-REQUIRED` (BP-037) and one is `DEFERRED` in its own words (BP-042).

**Rule 4 — the trailing pinned-runtime item is not re-charged to every entry.** Many entries from BP-002 to BP-040 close with the same line, that the Node 22.13.0 runner evidence is separate and pending (`:67`, `:199`, `:260`, `:266`, `:301`, `:327`, `:404`, `:469`, `:685`, `:735`, `:744`, `:799`, `:830`). That item belongs to BP-010 (`:226`, `:238–240`). It closed at `BP-041:845–846` for the tree of that date, and the Phase 5+ tree's own pinned-runtime evidence is `BP-063:1613–1620` (both platforms, `matchesPin: true`). It is counted once, at BP-010.

**Citations.** A bare `:NNN` is a line of `docs/断点记录.md`. Any other file is named in full. `FAILURE-FAMILIES.md`, `_INVENTORY-RAW.md` and the gate documents are cited by their line numbers.

## What this document is not

- It is not an audit of the runtime against the Bible, and not a re-test. No code, test run or CI artifact was executed or read for it; the only documents read beyond the three inputs are the ones the statuses point at (`PHASE-5.5-RECOVERY-GATE.md`, `PHASE-5-GATE.md`, `HANDOFF.md`, `release/PRE-RELEASE-GAP-MAP.md`).
- It is not a gate and it does not move one. No status here changes a gate result, and `CLOSED` is not a statement that a fix was re-verified — only that the record records a completed disposition and nothing later contradicts it.
- It is not the RFC mismatch register. The 24 registered labels are carried in `RFC-MISMATCH-RECONCILIATION.md`; they appear here only where a breakpoint's closure depends on one.
- It is not an amendment to the record, and it is not a substitute for §9 of `FAILURE-FAMILIES.md`, which answers a different question (which mechanism clusters with which).
